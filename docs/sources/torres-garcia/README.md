## What it is (and is not)

A narrative **legal and economic history** of Colombian money from the Congreso de Cúcuta (1821) to the 1935–1938 devaluation, written from a mid‑1940s perspective. It is the standard reference for **why** each monetary regime changed: laws, decrees, the metallic standards, and the *curso forzoso* (fiat) paper‑money era.

It is **not** a type catalogue. It carries **no Pick / BG# numbers, no serial ranges, no per‑type printages, and few plates.** For dated types, series letters, and *emisión* figures use González White (`../gonzalez-white/`); for issuer production totals use the BanRep tables (`../banrep/`); for photographs of banca‑libre / Banco Nacional / independence paper use the Heritage extract (`../heritage/`).

Because the text stops in the 1940s, do **not** cite it for anything after the 1938 devaluation (post‑war families, the 2016 family, polymer, etc.).

## How to use

1. Search the `.txt` for a law, year, standard, or institution (e.g. `Banco Nacional`, `curso forzoso`, `Kemmerer`, `Ley 167`).
2. Confirm the passage in the PDF (the `.txt` keeps hyphenation and line breaks from the original columns).
3. On a page, cite it as **Torres García, *Historia de la moneda en Colombia* (Banco de la República, 1945; ed. digital 2023), cap. N** — for historical context, not as a catalogue number or a printage.
4. When a figure (an emission ceiling, an amount authorized by a law) appears here, present it as what the law authorized, not as the printage of a specific note. Pair type‑level facts with González White or BanRep.

## Where it fits the catalogue

Strongest for the **early Colombia hub eras** that González White (1923–2019) does not cover:

- **Independence paper (1811–1819)** and the 1861–1863 *ensayo de papel moneda* (Cap. 4).
- **Banca libre and Banco Nacional (1870–1903)** — the metallic‑standard fights (Cap. 3, 5, 6) and the *curso forzoso* of 1886 (Cap. 7–9).
- **Banco Central de Colombia (1905)** and the run‑up to 1923 (Cap. 10–11).
- **Founding of the Banco de la República (1923)** and the Kemmerer reforms (Cap. 12).
