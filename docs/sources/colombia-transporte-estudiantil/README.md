## Classification (unchanged)

Popularly remembered and searched as a “billete.” **Technically it is not a banknote.** It is a printed **tiquete**, **vale**, **cupón**, or **boleto de control**. Closest site pattern is miscellaneous scrip (e.g. Baraboo 1933), not a BanRep type row. Do **not** invent Pick or González White BG#.

Possible mix-ups to keep off the site:

- Colombia **Decreto 188 de 1968** (12 Feb 1968, *Diario Oficial* 32.499) — military and police pay, not education. Signed Lleras Restrepo; ministers of Finance and Defence. [Pinzón Ortiz copy](https://www.jesuspinzonortiz.com/camara/basedoc/decreto/1968/decreto_0188_1968.html).

- Uruguay **Decreto 188/969** — civil-registry fund.
- Argentina **Decreto-Ley 18.188/69** — *peso ley*; the number is printed on Argentine **5 pesos** notes. Easy to conflate with “Decreto 188 de 1969” + “5”.

Arizmendi Posada **was** Minister of Education **2 Sep 1968 – 7 Aug 1970** under Lleras ([Wikipedia](https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Octavio_Arizmendi_Posada)). He signed education decrees that year (e.g. Decreto 1351 de 1969, MEN staffing, *Diario Oficial* 32.876). That does not make him the author of this ticket.

## Documented Colombian student-ticket / fare paper

Read the **printed issuer, date, and denomination** before picking a story. Several different objects exist.

### 1. Tranvía de Bogotá — 5-centavos cupones (late 19th / early 20th c.)

The Empresa del Tranvía Municipal de Bogotá had **5-centavo coupons**, each valid for one trip (animal-traction tram from 1884). Later ordinary tram fare is also cited at 10 centavos. A surviving **5 centavos** paper piece may be this coupon, not a 1969 subsidy ticket.

Source: engineering thesis on SITP fare validation, Universidad Distrital, citing the municipal tram company ([repository PDF](https://repository.udistrital.edu.co/bitstreams/75f0875f-9504-4617-9e04-d4f21adfe681/download)).

### 2. Tiquetes escolares on the tram (still in use 1949; tram ends 1951)

Schoolchildren had the right to ride **free** in academic periods. By 1948–49 the company restricted **tiquetes escolares**: girls’ tickets were accepted only on open cars; closed cars had required **two tickets** per trip. Last tram line ran **30 June 1951**.

Source: Sandra Patricia Salamanca Cruz, “Entre rieles y asfalto. Bogotá, transporte y vida urbana: 1938–1954,” *Universitas Humanística* 85 (2018), quoting *El Tiempo* “Sólo en tranvías abiertos pueden viajar las niñas de colegio,” 1949 ([PDF](https://revistas.javeriana.edu.co/index.php/univhumanistica/article/download/22471/19882/86602)).

### 3. Bogotá bus student tiquetes, January 1959 (best analog for “school + company tickets”)

After a Distrito Especial tariff resolution (reported *La República*, 6 Jan 1959):

- Ordinary urban fare: **10 → 25 centavos**.
- Students in establishments approved by the Ministerio de Educación: **10 → 12.5 centavos**.
- Benefit only on school days (not Saturday after 2 p.m., Sundays, holidays, or vacations).
- Student had to carry a **pase de identificación** from the school **and pay with tiquetes that the transport companies supplied through the schools**.

That is a printed student ticket used as proof of the reduced fare — the mechanism in the original brief — but the documented denomination is **12.5 centavos in 1959**, not 5, and the president is **Alberto Lleras Camargo**, not Carlos Lleras Restrepo.

Source: José Abelardo Díaz Jaramillo, “¡Abajo las oligarquías! Protestas contra el alza de las tarifas del transporte en Bogotá (1959),” *Revista Grafía* 14(1) (2017): 71–92, [https://doi.org/10.26564/16926250.761](https://doi.org/10.26564/16926250.761).

### 4. January 1969 — subsidy raised by **5 pesos**, including secondary and university students

This is the Lleras Restrepo / late-1960s episode. It is a **cash auxilio**, not a 5-centavos ticket:

- **2 Jan 1969**: urban fare increase; partial 24-hour strike (*El Tiempo*, 2–3 Jan 1969).
- **15 Jan 1969**: *El Tiempo* “Alzan subsidio de Transporte” — government raised the workers’ transport subsidy by **5 pesos** and **also increased that subsidy for secondary and university students**.
- **22 Jan 1969**: UTC/CTC strike against the fare rise; Labour Ministry negotiations.

A 5-centavos **face** on a 1969 student bus ticket would already have been far below the 1959 student fare of 12.5 centavos. Treat “5 centavos + Decreto 188 de 1969” as unverified until the piece is read.

Source for the 1969 subsidy: academic paper on Frente Nacional transport protests, citing *El Tiempo* 15 Jan 1969 pp. 1 and 9 ([Scribd copy of the ponencia](https://es.scribd.com/document/149903469/Ponecia-Para-Publicar-Como-eBook)).

## Legal frame (workers’ auxilio and company subsidy — not a student banknote)

These laws explain “subsidio de transporte” in the period. They do **not** by themselves identify the paper object:

- **Ley 15 de 1959** — State intervention in motor transport; employer **auxilio de transporte**; urban transport fund. Bogotá auxilio originally **$0.25 per trip** (art. 6), adjustable by the government. [Función Pública](https://www.funcionpublica.gov.co/eva/gestornormativo/norma.php?i=172765).
- **Decreto 1258 de 1959** — regulates Ley 15; Fondo de Subsidio paid to bus companies in the Distrito Especial. [Régimen Legal de Bogotá](https://www.alcaldiabogota.gov.co/sisjur/normas/Norma1.jsp?i=172957).
- **Decreto 1277 de 1971** (and Decreto 272 de 1972) — later national subsidy **to companies per vehicle-day**, to keep fares from absorbing all fuel-cost rises. Distinct from a student coupon.

Student **cash** subsidies today (TuLlave / DaviPlata in Bogotá; Metro *tiquete estudiantil* in Medellín) are later local programmes. Do not back-project them onto a 1960s stub.
